# Interior and exterior project illustrations

The built-in ImageGen tool produced three interior and three exterior comparison pairs. The contact sheets were split at their midpoints to create the twelve WebP files in this directory.

## Exterior studies

Make one SQUARE photographic diptych, two 2:1 landscape exterior photos stacked edge to edge top and bottom exactly halfway down the canvas. Each is an independent uncropped view that includes the full exterior subject. Same locked tripod, exact same horizon/ground plane, field of view, lens perspective, fixed landmark trees, adjacent details and same light direction in BOTH photos. Realistic premium editorial property photography in Winnipeg, Manitoba. Soft clear daylight, natural material response, subtle contact shadows, believable dimensions; ordinary Canadian utility details, no glow or stylization. No people, numbers, plaques, text, logos, typography, borders or gutters.

## Interior studies: shared direction

Create one photoreal two-panel contact sheet for a Canadian residential fire restoration website. SQUARE canvas exactly. Two equal wide landscape photographs stacked vertically, seam at exactly 50% of image height. No line, frame, divider, text, captions, signage, watermark or people. The upper photo is fire damage after extinguishment, lower photo is the exact same room restored. Match tripod position, framing, scale, perspective, permanent wall openings and window mullions in both panels. Both halves show the full room separately edge to edge. Professional but unglamourized Canadian residential architectural photography, neutral white balance, normal daylight shadows, true material texture and straight verticals, 30 mm full-frame lens. Top has locally credible varied soot, material damage and suppression water staining with an identifiable damaged area. No active fire, no theatrical flames. Bottom is a believable warm home photographed after repairs, edited with restrained natural colour, not a CGI showroom.
